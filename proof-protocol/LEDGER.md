## Lessons

1. Every flag the seed raised about its own citations was warranted, and in two of two cases the error was WORSE than flagged. A self-flagged low-confidence claim should be treated as probably wrong, not probably imprecise.
2. Three of the four wrong predictions made this session were mine, and two were caught only by an external check — three blind raters on N8, and a rule-less rater on the baseline. Self-scoring found the vocabulary bug; it did not find either of the substantive errors.
3. The predictions that held up best were the seed's about CONTAMINATION. The ones that failed worst were everyone's about DIAGNOSTIC VALUE. That asymmetry is the programme's central result so far: it is much easier to establish that a measurement is confounded than to establish that a procedure helps.
4. No prediction in this programme has yet been settled by a retrospective 2x2. They were settled by a dating check, an arithmetic check, a unanimous rater disagreement, and a baseline.
5. Twice now a criterion has been dropped for the same reason: no computable diagnostic (auxiliary-witness lift, and the E5(b) replacement). Both were phrased as insights rather than as procedures. A criterion that cannot say what it reads off the page is not a criterion, and the catalogue has shrunk from five transformations plus two proposals to two tested rules.
6. Pre-registering my own answer before the audit was worth more than the audit alone. It showed that self-analysis reached the right VERDICT on all three points but missed the two strongest ARGUMENTS on the decisive one. The pattern across this session is consistent: self-scoring finds bugs, external checks find substantive errors.
7. The rebuild produced four findings and no working rule. Every finding came from something BREAKING: a unanimous rater disagreement (N8), an adversarial audit (N9), a baseline (D25), and a simpler rival (D28). Nothing came from the rule working. Build the rival and the baseline FIRST next time — they are cheaper than the rule and they are what produces the findings.
8. Twice this session I set out to check another party's work and found my own error instead: the positive-class vocabulary bug, and now the Behrend coefficient. Both times the other party was right. Combined with N8 (three raters right, me wrong) and the N9 audit (auditor right on all three points), the pattern is now unambiguous: on this project my error rate on substantive checks is higher than that of the people and agents I am checking. Weight external results accordingly.
9. The two citation predictions in this ledger point the same way. P-23 bet that a survey would cover a paper outside its scope and lost; P-25 bet that a paper would state its own theorem and won. What closes rows here is naming the RIGHT document, not trying harder with the wrong one -- and the right document is almost always the primary one.
10. Rendering the markdown from JSON stops the prose drifting from the data, but it does not stop a hand-written field INSIDE the data from being wrong, and this one was wrong twice in a row in opposite directions. D19's action text listed four open citation rows and omitted a fifth that the data had always carried as UNVERIFIED (C1-gotsman); the replacement I wrote while closing control 15 then called three rows UNVERIFIED when later rows had already moved two of them to SEARCH and PARTIAL. Reading the sentence caught neither; a derived count caught both immediately. Counts that can be derived must be derived -- tools/citation_status.py now computes them, the standing caveat is generated from it, and tools/check_consistency.py fails the tree if the prose drifts again.
11. The free-text reasons in a rating sheet were worth more than the coded fields, and they had been sitting in d20/ unread since the run. Scoring the codes gave 'raters disagree on 6 fields'; reading the WHY fields gave 'in three of those six they state the same mathematics and are forced apart by the vocabulary'. Those are different findings with different remedies, and only the second is true. Every rating sheet in this programme must keep collecting the reason, and the reasons must be read BEFORE anything is concluded from the codes.
12. A cap computed from an invariant is a cap ON THE INVARIANT, not on the problem, and the instrument said otherwise for one commit. T3's first wording inferred 'no improvement to the move can reach O(1)' from a threshold that grows with n; a blind rater pointed out that on two of the six benchmark boards the incumbent move is itself lossy and the target is nonetheless TRUE (Marcus-Spielman-Srivastava for Kadison-Singer, Spencer for discrepancy), so the cap licenses 'replace the certificate', not 'the bound is unreachable'. The stronger reading is licensed only when the move is MONOTONE in the invariant -- of the three boards where the question parses, exactly one, Banaszczyk's measure board, where the move can only shrink measure. The general form of the mistake is reading a negative result about a proof technique as a negative result about the theorem, and it is the same error 6(b) of the same instrument was built to catch in someone else.
13. A number that cannot fall is not a measurement, and this programme published one for several commits. The transformable-board screen reported PRECISION 2/2 = 1.000, and when a blind rater reclassified a third of the corpus the figure did not budge -- every case in the scored set is a success whose board changed, so every fire is a true positive by construction, and the screen reads only the 2 items that have both its inputs. The tell was available without any rater: a screen whose scored set contains no negative cannot report precision at all. Look for it wherever a figure of merit is quoted here: ask what the number would have to see to go down, and if the answer is 'nothing in the corpus', delete the number rather than caveat it.
14. The triage questions passed a board that cannot work, twice, and the KILL TEST caught it both times. Arm 3's board cleared T1 (the invariant is checkable in closed form on the target) and cleared T3 (the threshold is O(1)), and is dead: directional total variation is exactly translation-invariant while 'the final body contains the origin' is not, so every legal run has a translate with identical invariant values at every step whose final body misses the origin by as much as you like. No amount of triage finds that, because triage asks whether each slot is respectable and this is a mismatch BETWEEN slots -- an invariant blind to the thing the terminal condition needs. The transferable rule: check the symmetry group of the invariant against the symmetry group of the terminal condition. If the invariant is invariant under a group the terminal is not, no preservation lemma can bridge them, and that is a one-line refutation available before any computation.
15. The first replication in this programme changed the finding, and not by contradicting a number. Two blind respondents on one input agreed on every computation -- the same cos^2 witness, the same Fisher information, the same projection bound -- and returned OPPOSITE VERDICTS, dead against not-yet-an-argument. The whole difference was how each read a slot both had marked `none recorded`: as no constraint, or as unspecified. A single run would have reported whichever verdict it drew as the result, with the agreement on the mathematics as corroboration. So the case for n = 2 is not that the second run might contradict the first's arithmetic; it is that a verdict can be determined by an instrument's silence, and one run cannot see its own instrument.
16. Twice in one session a check was written that could not fail, and the negative test caught both. The Epoch-gate matcher would have reported a false EXCLUSION: Epoch lists the rank of the 4x4 matrix-multiplication tensor and our candidate is the 3x3 tensor, and every cheap similarity measure fires on that pair. The fix was not a better similarity measure but a structural one -- screen wide, then refuse to return ANY verdict until every collision carries a written adjudication and a reason, and abort on two positive controls known to be on the list before the run. It caught two more false friends the same way: Conway's THRACKLE conjecture is also Conway's and is not the 99-graph, and 'classical factorization algorithms' is integer factorisation, not graph 1-factorisation. Then the dangling-citation check written later that day matched nothing at all, because a heredoc turned `\\.` into a literal backslash inside an r-string; it reported OK on a tree that had five dangling citations in it. In both cases the failure mode was identical and invisible from the passing output: the check ran, said what I wanted to hear, and was incapable of saying anything else.
17. The gate cleared its target and cannot score anything, because no prediction was registered before it ran. RESULT_GAP_v2 section 5 made the Epoch diff binding and even named the risk -- 'MOLS(10) and Costas 32/33 could both be on the unreadable forty' -- but never committed to a number. The result (0 of 4 ranked candidates on the list, 2 of 16 pool items on it) is therefore a measurement of Epoch's list and not of my judgement about it, and the difference is free to register and impossible to recover afterwards. The gate also paid for itself in a way worth separating from its verdict: two pool items were retired without the promoting search each would have needed, so a cheap lookup run FIRST removed work rather than merely licensing it.
18. The premise-falsification line the v2 rubric demands fired on the first target the rubric produced, and it cost one afternoon rather than one run. RESULT_GAP_v2 section 6 said 'symmetric-ansatz extension first' for PTE size 11; under the reading a reader meets first -- the half A is symmetric about its own mean, which is what the classic size-6 solution does -- that search space is PROVABLY EMPTY at every odd size, because the odd power sums vanish identically and Newton's identities then force the two halves to coincide. A naive implementation enumerates nothing and reports a null that means nothing. The recommendation survives only under the other reading, B = t - A. Two lessons, not one: a plan can be killed by an ambiguity in a single word rather than by an error, and the cheapest way to find out which reading is live was to SEARCH for the small solved cases and look at what symmetry they actually have -- six at size 5, none of the first kind, against eight of eight at size 4.
19. An exhaustive null is only as good as the region it covers, and the cheapest way to size that region is against a SOLVED case of the same shape. The size-11 search swept H <= 36 completely and found nothing; that reads like progress until you compute where the smallest known size-7 solution actually sits -- primitive centred height 51, diameter 102 -- and see that an hour of container time did not reach the height of a solution four sizes easier. Nothing in the run itself says this. It took one calibration against an object already in hand, and it turned a confirmed prediction into a result that carries almost no weight against the target. The rule: before reporting a null, locate the nearest solved instance inside your own coordinate system and say whether you reached it.
20. Convert an algorithmic win into the units of the frontier before deciding it matters. Cancado-Coutinho-Spier's Corollary 21 forces the sign pattern of an ideal PTE solution, and building it in cut the search by 17x -- verified against the old instrument on the same control, same two solutions out, 21.0 s to 1.2 s. That reads like a breakthrough until you notice the cost is C(H,6): a 17x speedup is 17^(1/6) = 1.6x in height, and the gap to the published frontier was 40-70x in height. The win was real, correctly measured, and worth about four percent of what was needed. The general rule: a constant-factor improvement to a search whose cost is the k-th power of the frontier parameter buys the k-th ROOT of that factor in frontier, so quote the improvement in frontier units before letting it change a decision.
21. I inferred a frontier was compute rather than method, from a citation rather than a paper, and said it confidently. The claim was that Coppersmith et al.'s height-3500 result at PTE size 11 must be about a hundred core-years, because C(H,6) at H=3500 is 2.5e18 and no cleverness was visible in the reference. The paper then showed their section 4.1 uses the divisibility structure as the GENERATOR of the enumeration rather than a filter on it -- every selected element forced into a residue class mod 31 or 29, so 2H/p candidates instead of 2H -- and their enumeration is 52,684 times smaller than mine at H=90. Worse, my own write-up had named that exact use case one paragraph earlier, as the architecture where a congruence sieve pays, and then concluded nobody had built it. The rule: when the only thing in hand is a number from a paper's abstract or a citation, the reachable claim is about OUR method, never about theirs; 'they must have spent compute' is a statement about a paper that has not been read.
22. The same prediction, made twice about the same quantity, was wrong when guessed and right when costed. A-2 and B-2 both claimed memory would bind before the clock in a meet-in-the-middle search. A-2 was written with no measurement behind it and failed -- the clock bound, with 5.8 GB spare against a 12 GB cap. B-2 was written after two ladders had measured 428 bytes per left-table key and 172k right-tuples per second, named the crossing point as H = 85, and was confirmed: H = 80 peaked at 10.3 GB and H = 90 died on the cap, with the true wall at 82-83. The rule is not 'predict less' but 'a mechanism claim is worth registering only once you can compute where the mechanism changes hands'; before that it is a guess wearing a prediction's clothes, and it spends the credibility of the ones that are costed.
23. Three curated problem lists, built independently by serious people, overlap pairwise at 5-20%, which makes 'absent from list X' nearly worthless as evidence. Measured: Epoch's 41 unsolved FrontierMath entries against ProofAtlas's 499-record importance ranking, 5 matches; this project's own 24 vetted topics against the 499, nine present and four usable; our pool against Epoch, two of sixteen. Fifteen of our topics -- skew-Hadamard, Williamson, Steiner systems, difference sets, Costas arrays, bent functions, APN, 1-factorisations, D-optimal designs, covering codes, PTE, cap sets, snake-in-the-box, Moore graphs, strongly regular graphs -- are absent from the 499 entirely. That is not a defect in anyone's list: an editorial ranking of canonical importance and a finite-witness screen are close to orthogonal selection functions. Two consequences. RESULT_GAP_v2's worry that the commons is being strip-mined is overstated, because the strip-miners are working disjoint seams. And the Epoch gate's CLEAR verdicts are weaker than they read -- clearing one list says little about the other three.
24. Provenance tiering measures where a claim came from, not how precise it is, and the two come apart in the direction that flatters the work. Measured on the same record twice by two different instruments. A cheap mapping pass over 106 corpus records returned, for record 235 (Zauner/SIC), a cell tiered PRIMARY and sourced to a real journal paper: exact fiducials 'reaching d=19,603'. True as far as it goes. A dedicated gate run earlier on that one record had already recorded the correction: 19,603 and 39,604 are the NUMERICAL maxima, the exact ceiling is 5,799, and the papers reaching the higher dimensions admit their exact verification was mostly partial. Same fact, PRIMARY tier both times, and a factor of 3.4 apart -- with the coarse version being the one that makes a search region look reachable. So the tier answered 'did you read a source' when the question a gate asks is 'is this value precise enough to start work against'. The rule: a breadth layer is triage-grade by construction. It is worth paying for -- it told us which records had a movable number at all, and it caught five resolutions we did not know about -- but no candidate may leave it without its own promoting search, and the tier on its cells must not be read as clearance. Add a resolution axis to the tiering, or read every breadth cell as SECONDARY regardless of what it cites. [CORRECTED 30 Sep 2026, board-finder/gap/discovery/RESULT_A2B_DIVERGENCE.md sec 2-3. The rule survives; two facts in the story do not. FIRST, the number was 5,779, not 5,799: 5,779 = 76^2 + 3 exactly, while 5,799 - 3 = 5,796 is not a square. The digit came from the relayed response, but GATE_RESULT_SIC.md's own check tested the n^2+3 form, found three of four dimensions fitting, met the one that did not, and INVENTED A SECOND FAMILY to absorb it rather than doubting the digit - an anomaly detected by a working check and then explained away, which is D49 and is a different failure from an unsourced number. SECOND, the attribution is backwards: 19,603 is not a breadth-pass artefact, it is the headline of the refereed JMP 2022 abstract, SICs in 13 prime dimensions of the form n^2+3 with the highest p = 19,603. The breadth pass relayed the refereed headline CORRECTLY. There are at least three senses in play - an exact expression conditional on conjectural Stark units, numerical verification to a stated precision (2,000 digits at 5,779, 1,000 at 19,603, 100 at 39,604), and contiguous versus sparse coverage - so the gate's 'the exact ceiling is 5,799' was itself a choice among senses presented as a fact. The lesson is therefore stronger than written and about a different party: it is not that a breadth pass is sloppier than a gate, it is that WHERE A LITERATURE CARRIES SEVERAL SENSES OF ONE QUANTITY, ANY SINGLE CEILING IS A SELECTION, and a gate that reports one without naming the others has not escaped the failure it was built to catch.]
25. A metric written after the data that falsified its predecessor is fit, not tested, and the only thing that can test it is a slice you have not run. The NONE-rate screen was registered over four chunks and falsified on chunk C; the replacement metric (the tracked quantity is a NUMBER and the mover is CONSTRUCTION or COMPUTATION) was then written and applied to A, B and C -- all three already read. Chunk D had not been run, so it was the one clean test available, and out of sample the metric behaved as it had in fit: 2 of 27 selected, both dead on a second criterion, no new candidate. Both of its clauses also turned out to be independently load-bearing, each rejecting records the other would have passed -- one record's mover was literally computer-assisted proof with no number to beat, while two others had crisp numeric thresholds moved only by proof technique. That is a real result. But the credit is smaller than it reads, and the reason is the lesson: I got the held-out chunk from the accident of processing order, not from reserving it, and I registered no bar for it before reading it. Had D selected eight records I could have called that vindication too. When a registered prediction fails and you replace the instrument, hold back a slice ON PURPOSE and write down what result would discredit the replacement, before you look.
26. A rubric label the literature never supplies is not a discriminator. g(gap_type) was operationalised as 'what caused the last move: a new idea, or the same method run bigger', and scored for the first time against outcomes (the 67-problem AlphaEvolve set). The COMPUTE class came back EMPTY: 0 of 10 testable problems, and essentially none across two other arms. The reason is structural, not environmental -- a published record move has to claim novelty, so no paper describes itself as the old method run longer. So the test that was meant to separate idea gaps from compute gaps actually separated 'move kind knowable' from 'unknowable'. What did the separating in the same run was crowding: which records an AI system had moved in the last year. Before registering a label as a predictor, check that the evidence source can produce every value of it.
27. Scope a crowding test to the object people actually attack, not the cell you happened to pick. Registered per record, the crowding gate passed kissing dimensions 13 and 14 inside a table that three AI systems had moved twice within twelve months, and two of the run's three survivors exist only because of that scoping. The same run found three of 19 corpus records moved in August-September 2026, two with AI involvement. The frontier this project is screening is moving on a timescale of weeks, so a triage cell is stale about as fast as it is written.
28. A verified computation licenses only the claim it computes, and the tier does not travel to the next sentence. costas_support.py proved -- assertions passing -- that no Welch or Golomb array reaches orders 31-33 by corner DELETION, and the next sentence concluded that order 31's known arrays are 'necessarily sporadic, found by search'. A dot can also be ADDED at a corner. The order-31 arrays are Welch p=31 plus one added dot, exactly 8 under the dihedral group, verified here. The 5.00-bit positive control that was the whole reason to reopen the cell never existed; the nearest search frontier is 43.21 bits away. The value was PRIMARY-VERIFIED-LOCALLY and the inference from it was ungraded, and it went into a prompt as 'Treat it as given' -- the strongest instruction in the document attached to its weakest link, while the relayed cells around it carried warnings and invited challenge. The run that was told to take it as given refused, and was right. Ask of any derived claim: what operators or cases does this quantify over, and did I enumerate them or assume them?
29. A replication is worth more than the verdicts it replicates, and this project got one by accident. Brief C was answered twice by independent Sonnet 5.5 instances. The two opened sixteen sources with an overlap of TWO, answered every question from a different paper, and returned the same verdict on four of five records -- so those four survive a near-total change of evidence base, which is the first robustness measurement any instrument here has had. The fifth split, and the split was the finding: both runs had identical evidence and identical hedges, and differed only in whether they converted that into a verdict, which exposed a registered prediction's threshold as unevaluable (D54). Two apparent numerical disagreements between the runs were checked and both were non-disagreements -- different constructions in one case, the same statement stated two ways in the other -- so 'the runs disagree' needed verifying before it could be reported, exactly like any other claim. Budget one replication per instrument before trusting a single run's REASONS, even where its verdict holds: the verdicts agreed and almost none of the supporting evidence did.
30. Before writing a brief, grep the local tree for the target's identifiers. Brief E v1 was written from a queue entry and a plan, and three of its findings were already in two documents two directories away -- one of them RETRACTED, so its single offered lead pointed into a cell this project had already withdrawn. The queue entry had made the same mistake first, one day after the document that answered it was committed. And I made mine one turn after registering D52, which is about the neighbouring problem, so writing the discipline down did not transfer it. The check is one command and it is not 'is this path valid' but 'has this question already been answered here'. What did survive: every arithmetic claim reproduced the repo's own independently, which is worth something and is not a finding.
31. 'Not known' is not 'cannot exist', and a retraction can overshoot as easily as a claim. HADAMARD_CROSS.md section 8 correctly inferred that W(179) and W(223) were not KNOWN -- H(716) and H(892) were open until 2026 and a Williamson quadruple of order m hands you H(4m) -- and then called the routes 'dead on arrival'. With W(65) still an open question the decided Williamson frontier is below 65, so W(103), W(179) and W(223) are undecided, not refuted, at distances of 38, 114 and 158 orders. The route is a search at a large distance: a cost, not an impossibility. Two separate near-misses in one document, in opposite directions -- the section 2-3 claim was too optimistic and the section 8 retraction too pessimistic -- which is a reason to read a retraction as critically as the thing it retracts. Also settled the same day: '2004 is the first open order above 2000' circulates on the web and is false, since 2003 is prime and 3 mod 4 so Paley I gives H(2004); the smallest is 2060.
32. An 'unswept' claim scoped to one instrument is worthless when the programme has others. Brief E's clause (c) asked whether FINDING_155's ranking was scoped to a single b. It was -- b=0 only, every row -- which by the brief's own logic made b >= 3 unswept. It was not: FINDINGs 62-69 had screened it with different instruments, entering 17 odd-b pairs and 135 configurations and finding nothing. I asked whether the RANKING covered the region when the question was whether ANYTHING had. That is D51's shape one level out -- D51 is that S4 counts moves so cannot see an attempt; this is that a negative scoped to one instrument reads as a negative about the world. Also from the same run: a brief shipped to an instance in ANOTHER repository cited paths relative to mine, and the tool built to catch broken citations validated them against the author's tree, which is the wrong tree. Resolvability is reader-relative and nothing here checked that.
33. Predict the KIND of failure, not its location. Two registered predictions in one session put confident commentary beside a threshold. P-38 guessed which of three clauses would fail and went 1 of 3, because that is a question about evidence not yet in hand. P-39 predicted the GRADE of each failure from structural features of the sources -- a 2012 paper will not document its compute in container-hours, so that bar fails UNEARNED for want of evidence; a specific numeric report is findable, so that bar fails ESTABLISHED on positive evidence -- and went 3 of 3. The thresholds held in both cases. What separates the commentary is whether it follows from the shape of the sources or from a guess about their contents.
34. Crowding cannot be measured from outside; it is measured by entering. Three bars on this board tested crowding by inferring contest from publication history, and D47 records that crowding decided none of them. Meanwhile the programme's own near-miss on a twenty-year-old conjecture was SCOOPED by another researcher a few weeks earlier -- a direct observation that the cell was contested in real time, available only because someone attempted it. S4 asks whether anyone has moved the cell; the decision-relevant fact is whether anyone is moving it now, and no amount of reading settles that. This is D51's observable problem with its answer attached: the state that matters is not in the literature, so a screen built on literature cannot see it.
35. Tiering a claim does not stop it doing work in the argument. I marked Chow's supposed remark -- that Conjecture 2 'might very well be false' and that he had searched for a counterexample without success -- as SEARCH-SUMMARY and explicitly not gate-usable, and then built a rhetorical case on it anyway, writing that the author had flagged the target and documented a null result. The paper says he believes the conjecture PLAUSIBLE and verified n = 3. The tier system governs what may pass a gate; it says nothing about what may shape a conclusion, and an untiered inference can carry a discounted premise at full strength. Check what a SEARCH-SUMMARY cell is doing in the argument, not just whether it is gating anything.
36. The binding constraint on a screen can be its corpus rather than its gates, and the two failure modes look identical from inside. Four readings of one retrodiction: first that SELECT v1's decomposition could not represent a conjecture-counterexample target, then that Theorem 4's ladder made it representable after all, then that the decomposition step was unregistered judgement -- and finally, once the scooping preprint arrived, that ALL SIX GATES PASS. 'The smallest rank at which C(3) fails' was a bracketed integer, 4 <= n <= 9, with both bounds on finite certificates: exactly the shape the screen is built for. It was never seen because it is in no table, and the corpus is a list of tabulated records. Seven screens over 499 records, and the one target that nearly worked was record-shaped and untabulated. Ask of a null screen result: did the gates reject these candidates, or did the corpus never contain them? The ledger records only the first.
37. The competitor for an LLM-assisted attack on a small open case is now another LLM-assisted researcher. Allikvere's preprint, which scooped this programme on Chow's C(3) by a few weeks, acknowledges Claude and GPT models used as assistive tools for 'proposing candidate constructions and draft arguments'. So the cell was not merely contested; it was contested by an independent researcher with the same class of tool, who shipped first. No literature-based crowding gate can see that contest until it resolves, which is why S4 and the C-1 and C-3 bars all measured the wrong thing -- and it means the window between a target becoming tractable to this method and someone else taking it is now measured in weeks.
38. Over-correction is a failure mode in its own right, and it looks like rigour. Told that a near-miss concerned 'the first counterexample of a twenty-year-old conjecture', I found Harvey-Kiraly-Lau's 2011 partial refutation and declared the framing wrong. Checked properly: the conjecture is twenty years old, its author published it as believed true while exhibiting counterexamples to his own weaker statements, HKL themselves called it open in the relevant range, and the refuting preprint frames its own contribution the same way. Only the word 'first' was arguable, and only technically. The new fact was worth having; the verdict I attached to it was not. Before calling a framing wrong, check whether the new fact actually contradicts it or merely qualifies it -- and note that the qualification here STRENGTHENED the retrodiction, since HKL's n >= 9 is the upper bracket that makes the target record-shaped.
39. A screen can be productive and score itself barren, and the tally will not tell you which. 43. A summary line is a claim about a document, not a substitute for it -- and the register built to enforce that rule broke it on its own first outing. SECOND, it is lesson 41 recurring one level down: 'specified' read exactly like 'performed', and now a modal 'may' read exactly like 'has not been'.
