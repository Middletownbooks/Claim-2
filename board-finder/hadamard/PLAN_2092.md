## 1. The target is 2092, not 2284

I proposed 2284 (*v* = 571) on the strength of `FINDING_05_target_ranking` — 156 free bits,
ranked first of thirteen. **That ranking is superseded inside the repo.** `FINDING_155` §3 rebuilt
it on a solution-density estimator validated against exhaustive counts at five small orders
(error ≈ 0.05 bits per equation), and concludes in its own words:

> **`v = 523` is the target.** … The ordering by difficulty is unambiguous:
> **523 ≺ 739 ≺ 659 ≺ 571 ≺ 643**.

| *v* | *N* | *d* | *r* | *D* | free bits | log₂ E[#sol] | density |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 487 | 1948 | 9 | 55 | 27 | 216 | 83.9 | 2⁻¹³² — **Alpöge solved this** |
| **523** | **2092** | 9 | 59 | 29 | 232 | 90.9 | **2⁻¹⁴¹** |
| 739 | 2956 | 9 | 83 | 41 | 328 | 117.0 | 2⁻²¹¹ |
| 659 | 2636 | 7 | 95 | 47 | 376 | 132.7 | 2⁻²⁴³ |
| 571 | **2284** | 5 | 115 | 57 | 456 | 172.8 | **2⁻²⁸³** |
| 643 | 2572 | 3 | 215 | 107 | 856 | 308.0 | 2⁻⁵⁴⁸ |

571 is **142 bits worse** than 523 and fourth of five. Why the two rankings disagree: FINDING 05
counted free bits at a fixed structure; FINDING 155 counts them at the best admissible orbit
decomposition and divides by the constraint cost. The later one is the better-founded.

**523's real appeal is that it is the nearest neighbour of the order Alpöge actually settled** —
9 bits of density, 2 equations and 16 free bits away from *v* = 487. That makes 487 a *positive
control with a known answer*, which this project has never had on this route.

### 1.1 An independent check of the parametrisation, since everything rests on it

The orbit parameter *d* must be an odd divisor of *v*−1, and the row-sum law
*a_k* = ±1 + *d*(2*u* − (*r*−1)) with Σ*a_k*² = 4*v* must admit a profile. Sweeping **every** odd
divisor and testing admissibility reproduces the repo's choice at all six orders:

```
 v=487  admissible d = [9, 3, 1]   largest d=9,  r=55,  D=27
 v=523  admissible d = [9, 3, 1]   largest d=9,  r=59,  D=29
 v=571  admissible d = [5, 3, 1]   largest d=5,  r=115, D=57
 v=643  admissible d = [3, 1]      largest d=3,  r=215, D=107
 v=659  admissible d = [7, 1]      largest d=7,  r=95,  D=47
 v=739  admissible d = [9, 3, 1]   largest d=9,  r=83,  D=41
```

Larger *d* dies on the row-sum law, not on taste: at *v* = 523, *d* = 29 leaves only
*a_k* ∈ {−1, +1}, so Σ*a_k*² ≤ 4 against the required 2092. **There is no unexplored *d*.** I
checked because a bigger *d* would have collapsed *D* and made everything below easy; it does not
exist.

## 2. The bottleneck, in the repo's own words

`FINDING_163` §5, one finding ago:

> The bottleneck is not probe coverage and not probe correlation. It is that **the walk reaches
> the floor about three times in seven minutes.** … **Make the descent reach and dwell at cost 32
> far more often.** This is the real problem and it is a landscape question, not an endgame
> question. Every enrichment attempted so far (depth-2, depth-4, depth-5) improved the probe;
> **none improved the rate of floor arrival.**

And the arithmetic behind the floor, from `FINDING_160` §1: residuals are multiples of 4 summing
to zero, so the smallest non-zero cost is two equations at ±4 — Σres² = 32. At *v* = 151 the probe
has fired **3298 times at the floor without closing**.

So there are two coupled failures, and findings 160–163 attacked only the second:

1. **Floor arrival is rare** — three visits per seven minutes.
2. **Each visit is one shot at a *D*-dimensional bullseye.** The depth-4 endgame asks: does any of
   the *M*⁴ ≈ 2.5 × 10⁹ one-swap-per-block combinations have delta **exactly** −res, all 15
   coordinates at once? Hitting one specified point of a 15-dimensional lattice with 10⁹ darts is
   the shortage FINDING 163 §1 measured at ~700×.

## 3. The proposal: the endgame is already a 10⁹-point neighbourhood, and it is being used as a
yes/no oracle

`FINDING_160` built an exact meet-in-the-middle: `P01 = A_i + B_j` over *M*² pairs,
`P23 = C_k + E_l` over *M*² pairs, hash-joined on `P01 = −res − P23`. It is validated (12/12 exact
recovery on perturbed solutions) and costs 0.01 s per probe.

**That machinery searches a neighbourhood of 10⁹ moves and is allowed to return only "solved" or
"nothing".** Make it return the *best* move instead:

> **Join on the support of res, not on all of *D*.** At the floor `res` has exactly two non-zero
> entries. Bucket `P23` by its values on **those two coordinates only**; for each `P01`, look up
> the bucket that zeroes them; score the survivors on the remaining *D*−2 coordinates and take
> the lowest Σres². Accept it as a **move**, not only as a solution.

What changes: the target goes from a point in 15 dimensions to a point in 2, so the bucket is
essentially never empty. Instead of one shot that almost always misses, every floor visit yields
a move that **kills the two frustrated equations exactly** and pays for it somewhere else — and
the cheapest such payment, over 10⁹ candidates, is usually another cost-32 state with a *different*
frustrated pair.

**That converts floor arrival from a rare event into a walk.** You stop needing to arrive at the
floor three times in seven minutes; you arrive once and then move *along* it, with a fresh
independent shot at exact closure at every step. It attacks precisely the landscape problem
FINDING 163 names, using machinery that is already built, already validated, and already fast.

**Cost.** At *v* = 151, *M* = 224 so *M*² = 50,176 vectors per side. Bucketing on two coordinates
gives ~10⁴ buckets and ~5 entries each, so ~2.5 × 10⁵ candidates scored per probe against the
current exact join's single hash lookup — a few milliseconds, the same order as the 0.01 s probe.
At *v* = 487, *M* = 729 and *M*² = 531,441, still small. **It scales to the target.**

**Risks, named in advance.** The floor-walk can cycle; tabu on the frustrated pair `(s₁, s₂)`
fixes that, and FINDING 163 §4 already observed pairs varying freely across restarts. And a move
that zeroes two equations may raise cost above 32; accept only non-increasing moves, or allow a
bounded uphill with the usual weighting.

### 3.1 Secondary, cheaper, and worth running first as a sanity check

`FINDING_163` §1 measured that at cost 96 the residual has support **6** in 1976 probes and
support **3** in 58. Support-3 states are structurally nearer to closing. The objective Σres² is
indifferent between them; **Σ|res|** is not, and neither is `Σres² + λ·|support|`. This is a
one-line change to the delta evaluation and it biases the descent toward the 58 rather than the
1976. It is not the same thing as the constraint weighting already in `swap_tabu.py`, which
reweights *equations*, not the *shape* of the residual.

## 4. The kill test, registered before any of it runs

This project has a ladder and a fitted law already: `log₂(seconds) = 0.503·(−log₂ density) −
16.9`, from *v* = 67, 163, 79, 131 (FINDING 157 §1). Re-fit it for the new descent on the same
four instances, under **FINDING 162's protocol** — seven seeds, matched caps, matched seeds,
medians — because that finding established that every single-seed timing in the project was
unsafe.

> **Single threshold, no middle band.** Extrapolate the re-fitted law to *v* = 487
> (density 2⁻¹³²). **Continue iff the extrapolated single-core time is ≤ 10⁸ s (≈ 3 years, i.e.
> about two weeks on 100 cores). Otherwise stop.**

For calibration: the current method gives 2⁴⁹·⁶ s ≈ 26 million years. The bar corresponds to a
growth exponent near 0.30 against the measured 0.503; FINDING 157 §3 computed that 0.252 would
put *v* = 487 at one day. **This is a half-day experiment that decides a months-long programme**,
and it is exactly the shape of test that this session's PTE work kept wishing it had run first.

If it passes: run *v* = 487 as the positive control before touching 523. The estimator says that
ansatz holds ~2⁸⁴ solutions and the project has never found one — so a failure there is a failure
of the engine, and a success is the first calibrated win on this route.

## 5. What I would not do, and why

- **Do not reverse-engineer Alpöge.** Extensively attempted, unsuccessfully. FINDING 157 §3
  already reframed the claim in the only units that matter: *if* it is a search, it has roughly
  half this project's growth exponent. That is a target for our own method, not a thing to
  recover.
- **Do not put the PSD filter back into CP-SAT.** FINDING 07 built it and FINDING 12 measured it
  as a **trap** at *m* = 515: three profiles that were `UNKNOWN` at 1300 s proved infeasible in
  2.4 s with the filter *off*. A 500× swing the wrong way.
  *(FINDING 157 §3 does name a genuinely untried variant — a **complete** search with monotone
  spectral pruning, hand-rolled rather than as CP-SAT rows. It is a real gap. But it is a larger
  build, and FINDING 163's diagnosis postdates it and points at the descent instead. Second arm,
  not first.)*
- **Do not enrich the endgame further.** Depth-2, depth-4, depth-5 are built; FINDING 163 measured
  that none of them moved floor-arrival rate. Depth-6 will not either.
- **Do not start at 2284.** 142 bits harder than 2092, on the repo's own validated estimator.
