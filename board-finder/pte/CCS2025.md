> Frederico Cançado, Gabriel Coutinho, Thomás Jung Spier. arXiv:2509.09948v1 [quant-ph],
> 12 Sep 2025.

Supplied by the user as a transcript. Everything below marked **verified** was checked against

## 1. The correction I owe first: this is a reduction *to* PTE, not a solver *for* it

The paper's own framing is explicit — *"we show that a particular case of this problem is
equivalent to a solution of a particular case of the well known Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem,
**deeming our original task certainly harder than anticipated**."* The direction of the
implication is: their quantum-walk question inherits PTE's hardness.

Theorem 18 gives the equivalence: for odd *d*, a *d*-chain with periodic cospectral vertices at
positions 0 and ⌈(d+1)/2⌉ exists **iff** PTE⁰ has a solution on ⌈(d+1)/2⌉ integers. For size 11
that is *d* = 21. But the chain side, unfolded through Proposition 13, is *"find monic p₁₁ with
|p₁₁| constant on the 22 integer zeros of p₂₂, strongly interlacing it"* — and that is
p_{S⁺} = p₁₁ − C, p_{S⁻} = p₁₁ + C, which **is** the PTE problem restated. Theorem 8 is the
bridge that makes the equivalence hold; it is not a device for searching.

So the paper does not hand over a faster route to an ideal solution of size 11. What it hands
over is a status statement, one constraint this project was not using, and a sharper name for
the target. Those turned out to be worth a great deal anyway.

## 2. It confirms the claim the whole target rests on

size 11 interesting is relayed, and everything verified here is about sizes that are already
solved."* The single load-bearing claim came from an LLM response.

The paper states it twice, in September 2025:

> §4.1 — *"Solutions for PTE_n are known only for n ≤ 10 and n = 12."*
> footnote 3 — *"Cases corresponding to n integers with 3 ≤ n ≤ 10, and for n = 12."*

**Read off the paper**, and that moves the claim from *"an LLM said so"* to *"a primary paper in
the area says so, and its whole point depends on it."* Size 11 is the smallest open case.

**It also corrects a relayed citation.** `sources/GAP_V2_RESPONSE_RAW.md` cited a 2023 paper by
Coppersmith, Mossinghoff, Scheinerman and VanderKam. Reference [5] of this paper is:

> in the Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem.* **Mathematics of Computation, 93(349):2473–2501, 2024.**

The authorship relayed to us was right; the **year was wrong by one**, and it is now a journal

## 3. The constraint this project was not using — and it is a large one

**Corollary 21** (the paper attributes it to Caley, *Thm. 2.1.3*): for an ideal solution with
*e*₁ < *f*₁,

> *e*₁ < *f*₁ ≤ *f*₂ < *e*₂ ≤ *e*₃ < ⋯ < *e*ₙ₋₁ ≤ *e*ₙ < *f*ₙ  (*n* odd)

i.e. the sorted union *E* ⊔ *F* falls into blocks of sizes 1, 2, 2, …, 2, 1 alternating between
the two sides. Equivalently, and this is the form worth having:

> **the element at sorted position *k* belongs to *E* if and only if *k* mod 4 ∈ {0, 1}.**

**Verified**, on every ideal solution this project can produce: 17 of 17 across *n* = 4, 5, 6, 7
— eight at size 4 (height ≤ 20), six at size 5 (height ≤ 30), two at size 6, and the size-7
classic. Not one exception, symmetric or asymmetric.

**Kleiman's divisibility** is in the paper too: if *E*, *F* solve PTE_n then (*n*−1)! divides
*p_E*(0) − *p_F*(0), which by Theorem 20 is the constant *C* = *p_E* − *p_F*. **Verified**, 17 of
17. But it is **weak in practice**, which is worth knowing before building on it — the observed
constants sit far above the bound:

| *n* | (*n*−1)! | smallest observed \|*C*\| | ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 6 | 180 | 30 |
| 5 | 24 | 5,040 | 210 |
| 6 | 120 | 100,800 | 840 |
| 7 | 720 | 13,967,553,600 | 19,399,380 |

So Kleiman is a sound check and a poor filter. Corollary 21 is the opposite.

## 4. What Corollary 21 does to the search

For a swapping-symmetric solution the union is *C* ∪ (−*C*), so the rule fixes, for each of the
*n* absolute values in increasing order, which side it is on:

> *s_i* = +1 if (*n* + *i*) mod 4 ∈ {0, 1}, else −1, and **C = { *s_i* · *u_i* }**.

For *n* = 11 that is (+, +, −, −, +, +, −, −, +, +, −): *C* has exactly **six positive and five
negative** elements, in a forced interleaving. **Verified** — the rule reproduces the centred
form of both size-5 solutions and the size-7 classic exactly, sign for sign.

The registered search in `PREREG_PTE11.md` §3 enumerated 11-element subsets of [−*H*, *H*] and
used none of this. The new one enumerates 11 increasing values in [1, *H*] and takes the signs
for free:

| | registered (`search_symmetric.py`) | pattern (`search_pattern.py`) |
|---|---|---|
| right-hand side at *n* = 11, *H* = 36 | C(73,6) = 170,230,452 | C(36,6) = **1,947,792** |
| *n* = 7 control at *H* = 60 | 21.0 s, 2 solutions | **1.2 s**, the same 2 solutions |

Same objects out, 17× fewer tuples in. Predictions B-1…B-4 for the new instrument are registered
in `PREREG_PTE11.md` §10, before its first size-11 run.

**The scope narrows in one way and it is not free.** The derivation needs *C* and −*C* disjoint,
which is exactly PTE⁰ — the variant Theorem 18 needs for odd *d*. Solutions with a repeated
element (the paper's PTE¹, and plain PTE) are not covered and stay with the old search.

## 5. What is still missing for A-4

The paper gives the reference but not the number. A-4 asks for the **height bound** of the
published exhaustive symmetric size-11 search, and neither Cançado–Coutinho–Spier nor its
bibliography states one. The promoting search still has to read Borwein–Lisoněk–Percival (2003)
and Coppersmith–Mossinghoff–Scheinerman–VanderKam (*Math. Comp.* 2024) for a covered region.
