3. The claim a finite deliverable does NOT make checkable
============================================================================
  'Twelve Hadamard matrices of previously open order' is two claims:
    (A) each is Hadamard of its stated order   -- decidable, seconds
    (B) those orders were previously open      -- not decidable here

  (B) rests on the Hadamard order tables: which orders already had a known
  matrix. That is a literature question. No amount of finiteness in the
  deliverable touches it, and it carries ALL of the novelty.

  Arms AT and AU scored artifacts by 'load-bearing claims verified' and
  counted only (A)-type claims. On this artifact that score would read
  12/12 while the whole point of the paper went unchecked.
  PASS  the two claim types are separated for the first time in this programme   [AT/AU conflated them]

5. What the proxy does and does not buy
============================================================================
  BUYS: given a claimed order n, this decides in milliseconds whether n is
  reachable by Sylvester, Paley I, Paley II and Kronecker products. An
  order NOT in that closure is one the standard toolkit cannot supply, so
  a matrix at such an order is at least not trivially old.

  DOES NOT BUY: whether the order was OPEN. Many unreachable orders have
  long-known matrices from other constructions -- Williamson, Baumert-
  Golomb-Hall, Turyn, Goethals-Seidel, cocyclic and computer searches.
  92 is the classic case: outside the Paley closure, and settled in 1962.
  The proxy is a NECESSARY condition for novelty, never a sufficient one.

  So the honest statement for such an artifact would be:
    (A) verified, 12/12, in seconds, with no provenance needed;
    (B) unverified here; n of 12 orders lie outside the classical closure,
        which is consistent with novelty and does not establish it.
  PASS  the proxy is stated as necessary-not-sufficient

6. The sharpened claim
============================================================================
  AU narrowed AT to: a FINITE DELIVERABLE is the variable. This case
  sharpens it again. The variable is not finiteness but whether the
  artifact's claim is COEXTENSIVE WITH A DECIDABLE FINITE CHECK:

    Hadamard   claim = 'this matrix is Hadamard'   check decides it    100%
    SSUF       claim = 'no good routing is cheap'  8 routings, all      100%
    Chow       claim = 'no valid grid exists'      1296 grids, all      100%
    CDC        claim = 'EVERY bridgeless graph'    15 graphs sampled      0%
    Nevanlinna claim = 'this function is not BT'   scaffolding only       0%

  CDC is the discriminating row: its artifact is finite in every instance
  and its claim is universal, so no enumeration can close it. A
  counterexample or construction has the coextensive property by nature --
  the claim IS the object. A universal theorem never does.

  That is why 'ship a finite object' is not advice one can always follow:
  it is available exactly when the target is an existence claim.
  PASS  the variable is restated as coextensiveness, not finiteness
