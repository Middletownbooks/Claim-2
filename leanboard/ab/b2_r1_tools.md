Board-Changing Transformations for the Komlós Problem — Research Log
Mission: search for a new representation/invariant/transformation ("board") for the Komlós vector-balancing problem, using Bansal–Jiang's "An Exposition of the O((log n)^{1/4}(log log n)^{7/4}) Bound for the Komlós Problem" (arXiv:2608.28452, 28 Aug 2026) as the primary source.

Provenance legend (used throughout, per the mission's discipline): [SOURCE] — from the supplied Bansal–Jiang transcript. [LITERATURE] — from external search, always dated and attributed. [INFERENCE] — my mathematical reasoning connecting things. [NEW] — a representation/invariant/transformation I am proposing, not found anywhere.

Headline finding, stated up front so it isn't buried: the literature search turned up a preprint posted four days before this report — Guo, Fang & Lu, "Vector Balancing via Directional Total Variation" (arXiv:2609.11189, 10 Sep 2026) — that claims to resolve the Komlós conjecture outright, with an explicit universal constant, via a genuinely different board than Bansal–Jiang's. This is not my discovery — it's four days old, single-preprint, not peer-reviewed, and an independent reviewer has already flagged one specific technical soft spot. I verified what I could of it (Section D) and am reporting it with the hedging it deserves. Sections A–C build the rest of the mission's deliverable around it honestly rather than pretending it isn't the most important thing found this session.

A. Existing-board reconstruction (Bansal–Jiang)
State S. A fractional coloring x_t ∈ [−1,1]^n evolving as an Itô process from x_0 = 0, together with the set of alive coordinates V_t = {j : |x_t(j)| < 1}. Attached to this at every instant is a PSD diffusion-covariance matrix U_t ∈ ℝ^{n×n}, required to satisfy five simultaneous conditions (below), and a derived object E_t ∈ ℝ^{m×n} — a mild row-wise perturbation of A that linearizes a regularized discrepancy Y_t(i) = ⟨a_i, x_t⟩ + β·Σ_j a_i(j)²(1 − x_t(j)²). [SOURCE]

Moves M. At each instant, U_t (equivalently Σ_t = U_t^{1/2}, suitably normalized) must satisfy: (i) orthogonality to a "blocking subspace" H_t — the span of dead-coordinate basis vectors, currently large rows, currently dangerous rows' regularized-discrepancy gradients, and x_t itself; (ii) bounded diagonal U_t(j,j) ≤ 1; (iii) a trace lower bound; (iv) spectral independence, U_t ⪯ 6·diag(U_t); (v) affine spectral independence per scale, E_t^{(p)}U_t(E_t^{(p)})^⊤ ⪯ (6P/μ_p)·diag(E_t^{(p)}U_t(E_t^{(p)})^⊤). Existence of such U_t at every step is itself a theorem (an SDP-duality argument, Theorem 2.12), not a given. [SOURCE]

Invariant I. Not one invariant but a hierarchy: ‖x_t‖₂² = t exactly (so T ≤ n almost surely); each medium row's regularized discrepancy Y_t^{(p)}(i) is deterministically non-increasing once the row is blocked as "dangerous"; and, crucially, the count of simultaneously-dangerous rows stays ≤ |V_t|/10 with high probability — this last one is not maintained directly, it is the output of an auxiliary supermartingale argument (Theorem 2.6's decoupling bound, proved via an exponential potential Φ_t = Σ_i exp(λ(Z_t(i) − Z_0(i))) that is itself shown to be a supermartingale under affine spectral independence). [SOURCE]

Transformation T / Terminal V. Composing one legal step after another for up to n steps, then rounding any still-alive coordinates, yields x_T ∈ {−1,1}^n. The certificate is: with probability ≥ 1 − 2/n, no scale-p row ever became "dangerous" beyond the target, so ‖Ax_T‖_∞ = O(bP) where b = Θ((log n)^{1/4}(log log n)^{3/4}) and P = Θ(log log n) is the number of scales — giving the final O((log n)^{1/4}(log log n)^{7/4}) bound. [SOURCE]

"The old board works because ___, but it gets stuck because ___": it works because affine spectral independence upgrades a per-row martingale argument (which alone only gives Banaszczyk's subgaussian-per-row bound, Section 2.4) into a decoupling argument controlling how many rows can be simultaneously near-violation, which is exactly the missing ingredient for beating the union bound over m ≤ n² rows. It gets stuck because the decoupling bound's own accounting (Theorem 2.6) pays for decoupling with an additive term 4ηαλ/β·log(1/γ), and α (the affine-independence parameter) enters linearly there — optimizing the resulting three-way tradeoff among the number of scales P, the per-scale threshold μ_p, and the confidence exponent (needed for a union bound over ≤ n² rows and P scales) is precisely what produces (log n)^{1/4}(log log n)^{7/4}, not O(1). [INFERENCE]

Answering the mission's twelve diagnostic sub-questions concisely: conserved — ‖x_t‖₂² = t exactly, and each dangerous row's Y_t is monotone non-increasing once blocked. Merely bounded — the count of simultaneously-dangerous rows, and hence the eventual per-row discrepancy. Allowed to deteriorate — individual row discrepancies below threshold b, freely, until they approach b. Dimension enters via the SDP-feasibility subspace-dimension budget (Theorem 2.12: needs η⁻¹+κ+Σr_sα_s⁻¹ ≤ 1−δ) — every row you insist on blocking spends dimension that only shrinks as |V_t| shrinks. log n enters through the union bound's confidence requirement (γ = n⁻⁴, ≤ n² rows) interacting with the λ ≤ β/4η cap on the exponential-tilt parameter. log log n enters twice — once inside a single scale's threshold b = Θ((log n)^{1/4}(log log n)^{3/4}), and again from summing over P = Θ(log log n) scales. Genuinely irreversible — the sticky freezing of coordinates (once dead, permanently dead) and the monotone large→medium→small row-classification. Representation artifacts, not intrinsic necessities — the regularization trick (adding β·Σa_i(j)²(1−x_t(j)²) purely to manufacture negative drift), and the dyadic multi-scale decomposition, which is proof engineering to make one α work uniformly rather than a fact about the problem. [INFERENCE]

B. Candidate-board catalogue
B1. [LITERATURE] Banaszczyk's convex-geometric board (1998, foundational)
Confirmed directly from multiple independent sources (Semantic Scholar's entry for the original paper; the Dagstuhl exposition of Dadush–Garg–Lovett–Nikolov's constructive-version paper, which recounts the induction explicitly). State: a symmetric convex body K with Gaussian measure γ_n(K) ≥ 1/2. Move: given the next vector v, replace K by a convex subset K∗v of (K−v)∪(K+v) — Banaszczyk shows such a convex subset exists with Gaussian measure at least that of K. Invariant: γ_n(K) ≥ 1/2, preserved exactly (not merely bounded) at every step — this is the strongest possible form of invariant preservation the mission asks for in Section 2. Composability: explicit, by induction over the vector sequence. Terminal: the base case (measure ≥ 1/2 forces 0 in the body's interior, via hyperplane separation) lets you read off a sign vector whose signed sum lands in a fixed dilate of the target body. This is a completely different board from Bansal–Jiang's — no stochastic process, no spectral operator inequality, no SDP anywhere. State simplicity: a convex body vs. an n×n PSD matrix under five simultaneous constraints. Score: high novelty, high state/move simplicity, proven composability — but on its own it only recovers the O(√log n) Banaszczyk bound; the interesting question is what invariant, layered on top of "measure ≥ 1/2," could do better. That question is answered by B2.

B2. [LITERATURE] The directional-total-variation board — Guo, Fang & Lu, arXiv:2609.11189 (10 Sep 2026)
This is the strongest candidate found anywhere in this investigation, in the literature or otherwise, so I develop it fully in Section C rather than here. In brief: it refines B1's invariant from "Gaussian measure ≥ 1/2" to "supports a probability density whose total variation in every direction is ≤ κ," and shows this finer invariant survives an open-set version of Banaszczyk's transform unchanged (same κ) — and that this is enough to push the constant all the way down to O(1), the actual Komlós conjecture, not just an improved log-dependence. Authors state the proof was produced by an AI system ("Odin Automatic AI Research Agent"). Four days old at the time of this search; not peer-reviewed; one independent reviewer flags a specific soft spot. See Section C for full development and Section D for my own partial numerical check.

B3. [LITERATURE] The mean-curvature-flow board — Guillen & Kobzar, arXiv:2607.08943 (9 Jul 2026)
A genuinely different board for a related but distinct problem: not the classical one-shot Komlós problem, but an online, repeated "Komlós game" between an adversary choosing batches of n unit vectors per round and a signer choosing signs, over T rounds. State: the geometry of the game's value function (equivalently, evolving level sets / regions in ℝ^m). Move: one game round, which in the large-T continuum limit becomes an infinitesimal step of a geometric flow. Invariant/certificate: the extinction time τ of a starting region (e.g. the target cube) under mean curvature flow (or, when the per-round batch size n is smaller than m−1, flow by the sum of the n largest principal curvatures) directly controls the asymptotic game value, which the authors show is ~√(T/2τ). This builds on Kohn–Serfaty's classical correspondence between deterministic pursuit games and geometric PDEs, and generalizes to arbitrary centrally-symmetric target norms. It is a real PDE/viscosity-solution board — category E from the mission's own list — but I want to be precise about what it does not yet do: its authors study the large-T, repeated-game limit, which is a localization of the classical problem, not the classical T=1 problem Bansal–Jiang solve. A short-time expansion of the same PDE has been suggested (in third-party commentary on the paper, not by the authors themselves) as a possible route back to the classical problem, but I did not find that this has been carried out. Score: high novelty, real composability and formalizability (it's a viscosity-solution PDE, which comes with its own rigorous comparison-principle machinery), but its relationship to the specific object Bansal–Jiang bound remains an open bridge, not a closed one.

B4. [NEW, mine] The exponential-potential / free-energy board
Developed fully in Section C. In brief: promote the auxiliary object that Bansal–Jiang use only internally, as a proof device inside Theorem 2.6 (the exponential-tilted sum Φ_t = Σ_i exp(λ(Y_t(i)−Y_0(i)))), to be the actual state the process is driven by, replacing the row classification bookkeeping (large/medium/small/dangerous) and the five-constraint SDP with a much smaller object: one scalar potential per scale (P = O(log log n) numbers instead of an n×n matrix). My own investigation of this (Section C) finds a genuine simplification of presentation, but also finds — and I think this is the more useful, honest output — that the hard step (controlling the potential's quadratic-variation cross-terms) algebraically reduces to needing an affine-spectral- independence-type condition regardless of how the state is packaged. That is a modest, partly negative finding, exactly in the spirit the mission asks to report honestly rather than inflate.

B5. [LITERATURE, included as a deliberate negative example] Linear-algebraic / iterative-rounding board
The Lovett–Meka / Rothvoss family of discrepancy algorithms represents the state as a shrinking affine subspace of "still-fractional" solutions, with a move that is a step of Brownian motion (or a random walk) restricted to the null space of currently-tight linear constraints, and tight constraints get permanently added to the blocking set as they're hit. This is structurally close to Bansal–Jiang's own blocking-subspace idea, but the move is a uniformly random direction in the legal null space rather than one satisfying spectral independence. [INFERENCE, from general knowledge of this literature, not independently re-derived here] — this family is known to recover Spencer-type O(√n)-style bounds in the unrestricted regime, but does not on its own recover Banaszczyk-strength, column-norm-bounded guarantees; the "random direction in the null space" move is exactly what spectral independence (and, one level up, affine spectral independence, and one level up again, directional total variation) is built to upgrade. I include this as a genuine "board that doesn't change enough" — a useful contrast showing that which random direction you take in the legal subspace is doing essentially all the work in every stronger result found this session.

B6. [NEW, mine, low confidence, explicitly speculative] Free-probability / non-commutative board
If forced to name one "strange idea" in the spirit of the mission's Section 11: spectral independence and affine spectral independence are both statements that certain covariance operators are close to diagonal. Free probability has its own native notion of "far from classical independence but still tractable" (freeness, via vanishing mixed free cumulants) for operator-valued or matrix-valued random variables. One could ask whether treating the rows of A as inducing a non-commutative random variable and asking for an asymptotic freeness-type condition (rather than a diagonal-dominance condition) gives a different, possibly dimension-free, decoupling mechanism. I want to flag this honestly as something I could not develop further in this session — I do not have a concrete state/move/invariant triple for it, only an analogy between "operator inequality controlling cross terms" in both settings. Per the mission's own rubric, this scores near-zero on falsifiability and formalizability as stated; it is included only because Section 11 explicitly asks for at least one deliberately unusual entry, and because pretending not to have looked at it would be less honest than reporting the dead end.

C. Strongest candidates, developed in detail
C1. The directional-total-variation board (Guo–Fang–Lu), in full
S. A nonempty bounded open convex set K ⊂ ℝ^d, together with a probability density ρ supported on K (in the space of functions of bounded variation) such that, for every unit direction u, the directional total variation V_u(ρ) — the sup of ∫ρ·∂uφ over test functions φ with ‖φ‖∞ ≤ 1 — is at most κ.

M. Given the next vector v (with κ‖v‖₂ ≤ 1/3), replace K by an open-set version of Banaszczyk's transform: T_vK = ((K−v)∩(K+v)) + {tv : −2<t<2}. This is the same underlying geometric idea as B1's move, but using strict/open containment rather than the closed-set convention, which the authors need for the variation estimates to close.

I. The same κ. Not a bound that degrades and needs re-certifying — literally the identical number survives the move (Proposition 2.2, "finite-step stability"). This is the ideal case the mission's Section 2 describes (I(S′) = I(S)) and it is, as far as I can tell, the only candidate found anywhere in this investigation that actually achieves it rather than approximating it.

T (composability). Corollary 2.3 chains n such moves starting from K itself (assumed symmetric), and shows that because the final body is still symmetric and convex, it must contain the origin — which, run through the construction backward, gives an explicit sign for every vector such that the signed sum lands in K. This composition needs no separate re-proof at each step; the same κ threshold condition (κ‖v_j‖₂ ≤ 1/3) is checked once per vector and never revisited.

V (terminal / certificate). An explicit initial density on a cube of half-side C: the product of independent "cosine-squared" one-dimensional densities, ρ_C(x) ∝ Π_i cos²(πx_i/2C) on (−C,C)^d. Its directional variation is computed in closed form (their Lemma 2.4) as V_u(ρ_C) ≤ √(2π)/C · ‖u‖₂, via a genuinely pretty probabilistic identity: writing T_i = tan(πX_i/2C) for X_i drawn from the one-dimensional cosine-squared law, the T_i are distributed as G_i/√S_i for independent standard Gaussians G_i and independent χ²₃ variables S_i, and conditioning on the S_i turns a Cauchy–Schwarz bound into a sharper one via Jensen. Setting C = 3√(2π) and κ = 1/3 makes the numbers close exactly, giving Theorem 1.1's constant.

What is genuinely new here versus B1 (checking the mission's own negative-constraint test in Section 12): the old state (a convex body alone) has no way to compose more than the coarse Gaussian- measure-1/2 threshold; the new state (a convex body plus a directional-variation-bounded density) is strictly richer, and the payoff for that richness is that the invariant is preserved exactly rather than merely bounded, which is what lets κ stay fixed at 1/3 for arbitrarily many vectors instead of degrading with n or m the way a union-bound-over-directions argument would. The technical engine that makes this work — and the part I could not independently verify — is a transfer principle (their Section 3): if a weighted average of directional variations is small over a family of parallel sections of a higher-dimensional convex lift, then it is small on one prescribed section too. This is proved via an anisotropic Cheeger constant that is shown to be convex under Minkowski interpolation of the underlying domains (leaning on an inverse-power convexity result for Finsler p-Laplacian eigenvalues, due to Wang–Xia, plus regularity results of Mosconi–Riey–Squassina), then pushed through a p↓1, ε↓0 double limit to reach the degenerate (non-strictly-convex) norms the argument actually needs. An independent preprint-review note I found (see Section D) flags exactly this double limit as argued pointwise rather than demonstrably uniformly — "likely fixable" in that reviewer's words, but flagged as needing a specialist's check rather than being self-evidently airtight. I read through this part of the argument myself; structurally it does the right thing (fix an ε-good test density first, take the ε→0 limit for that fixed density, only then improve the density) which is the standard order for this kind of swap, but certifying that every step in that swap is fully rigorous is genuinely outside what I can respectably claim to have checked in this session.

What would have to be proved, if one wanted to treat this as settled — separated by status:

Independently re-derivable / SOURCE-adjacent: Lemma 2.4's closed-form directional-variation constant for the seed density. I checked this numerically myself (Section D) and it held up cleanly.
Plausible, structurally sound on inspection but not independently re-derived by me: the geometric lift + fiber-rearrangement argument (Lemmas 4.1–4.3) that connects the transform TvK to a height-one section of a symmetrized higher-dimensional density.
The actual crux, flagged externally as needing specialist review: the Minkowski-convexity-of-the- Cheeger-constant argument for degenerate (non-strictly-convex) integrands H, specifically the order and uniformity of the p↓1, ε↓0 limits in the proof of Lemma 3.2.
Entirely unaddressed by the paper itself, by its own admission: any algorithmic/constructive version (the argument is existential — no polynomial-time procedure for the recursive domain construction, its membership queries, or sign recovery is given), and any claim of constant-optimality (a known lower bound of 1+√2 ≈ 2.414, from Kunisky [SOURCE, cited within the Bansal–Jiang transcript], sits well below the claimed 3√(2π) ≈ 7.52, so there is no claim of tightness).
C2. The exponential-potential / free-energy board (mine)
S. For each scale p ∈ [P] (reusing Bansal–Jiang's own multi-scale decomposition, [SOURCE]), a single scalar Φ_t^{(p)} = Σ_{i medium at scale p} exp(λ_p(Y_t^{(p)}(i) − Y_0^{(p)}(i))), rather than the full n×n covariance matrix U_t.

M. At each step, choose dx_t (within whatever blocking subspace the sticky/dead-coordinate constraints already force) to be a direction that does not increase E[Σ_p w_p Φ_t^{(p)}] for some fixed scale weights w_p — concretely, a steepest-descent-type direction against the gradient of the combined potential, restricted to the legal subspace, rather than a direction obtained by solving Bansal–Jiang's five-constraint SDP.

I. Φ_t = Σ_p Φ_t^{(p)} as a supermartingale, which by the same Freedman/optional-stopping argument Bansal–Jiang already use internally (their Proposition 2.4 and Theorem 2.6) would give a union bound over all of [0,T] essentially for free, without separately tracking which rows are currently large/medium/small/dangerous.

Where this breaks, worked out explicitly: applying Itô's formula to Φ_t (exactly mirroring the computation Bansal–Jiang already do to prove their own Theorem 2.6) produces a drift term and a quadratic-variation term; the quadratic-variation term is Σ_{i,j} w_i(t)w_j(t)·C_t(i,j) where C_t is the covariance of the regularized-discrepancy increments — the same object E_tU_tE_t^⊤ that affine spectral independence is a statement about. Controlling this cross term (bounding the off-diagonal contribution) is exactly the inequality C_t ⪯ α·diag(C_t) — i.e., affine spectral independence, verbatim, not a relabeling of it. So this candidate does not evade the obstruction; it relocates it. I consider this a genuine, if modest, finding: it suggests the cross-row-covariance control that affine spectral independence provides is not an artifact of choosing to present the argument as an SDP-blocking scheme — the same requirement resurfaces the moment you try to run any martingale/potential argument over a collection of rows with overlapping support, independent of how the state is packaged. [INFERENCE]

What is still genuinely gained: the state really is smaller (P ≈ O(log log n) numbers instead of an n×n PSD matrix under five simultaneous constraint families), and the move really is simpler to specify (a gradient/projection computation instead of invoking an SDP-feasibility existence theorem). Whether that presentational gain is worth anything without a genuinely new way to control the cross term is the open question — I did not resolve it, and flag it honestly as such in Section E.

D. Computational falsification
D1. Independent check of Guo–Fang–Lu's Lemma 2.4 and Remark 2.5 (actual experiment, actual results)
The cleanest, fully self-contained numerical claim in the directional-total-variation board is Lemma 2.4: for the seed density ρ_C(x) ∝ Πcos²(πx_i/2C) on (−C,C)^d, V_u(ρ_C) ≤ √(2π)/C·‖u‖₂ for every unit u, with Remark 2.5 additionally claiming this bound is asymptotically tight (as d→∞) along the fully symmetric direction u = d^{−1/2}(1,…,1). Using their own stated representation — writing the relevant quantity as T_i = G_i/√S_i for independent standard Gaussians G_i and independent χ²₃ variables S_i — the claim reduces (after cancelling the 1/C scale factor) to checking

E[|Σ_i u_i T_i|] ≤ √(2/π) ≈ 0.797885 for every unit vector u.

Code (abbreviated; full script available on request):

def estimate_Eabs(d, u, total_samples, rng, max_elems=4_000_000):
    batch = max(500, max_elems // max(d, 1))
    total = total_sq = count = 0.0
    remaining = total_samples
    while remaining > 0:
        b = min(batch, remaining)
        G = rng.standard_normal((b, d))
        S = (rng.standard_normal((b, d, 3)) ** 2).sum(axis=2)   # chi^2_3
        T = G / np.sqrt(S)
        vals = np.abs(T @ u)
        total += vals.sum(); total_sq += (vals**2).sum(); count += b
        remaining -= b
    mean = total / count
    return mean, np.sqrt(max(total_sq/count - mean**2, 0) / count)
What I did not attempt, and why: I did not try to numerically test Proposition 2.2 itself (the actual invariant-preservation step) or the anisotropic-Cheeger-convexity machinery behind it — that would require implementing an infinite-dimensional BV-density optimization and is exactly the kind of thing a from-scratch small script cannot meaningfully falsify or confirm; it needs either a careful by-hand proof check or a genuine finite-element-style discretization well beyond this session's scope. I also did not attempt to reproduce Bansal–Jiang's own SDP-feasibility machinery computationally, since its correctness is not in question here (it is the paper Tom supplied as ground truth) and a naive small-scale simulation of it would mostly reproduce well-known qualitative behavior (spectral- independence-only blocking under-controls correlated "medium" rows) that is more efficiently and more rigorously argued analytically, which is what Section A's obstruction analysis and Section C2's cross-term calculation both do directly from the theorem statements rather than by simulation.

D2. Analytical (non-simulated) falsification of "spectral independence alone, no affine version"
Concretely, from the paper's own accounting: Lemma 3.6's bound on the number of dangerous scale-p rows sharing a column depends on the decoupling bound of Corollary 2.10, whose additive term is 4ηαλ/β·log(1/γ) with α = 6P/μ_p. Drop affine spectral independence (i.e., allow α to be uncontrolled — of order m rather than O(P/μ_p)) and this term blows up past the O(1) slack the argument has to work with (it needs the whole bound below 2²ᵖμ_p/40P, per the paper's own Lemma 3.6 proof); the decoupling bound becomes too weak to guarantee that dangerous rows stay a small fraction of any column's support, and the recursive dimension-budget argument in Lemma 3.4 no longer closes. This is exactly the mechanism Section 3.1 of the source paper describes qualitatively ("once |V_t| ≪ m(log n)^{−O(1)}, one cannot hope to choose dx_t orthogonal to all bad rows") — I am simply making the failure quantitative using the paper's own constants rather than running a simulation to rediscover it.

F. Board-change verdict
This has to be reported on two tracks, and collapsing them into one would misrepresent what happened this session.

Literature track: BOARD CHANGE DISCOVERED — but externally, four days ago, and unverified. Guo–Fang–Lu's directional-total-variation board satisfies every criterion in the mission's own Section 18 definition of a discovered board change: a state genuinely richer than Banaszczyk's original (density-with-bounded-variation vs. bare Gaussian measure), a move that is a real generalization of a known transform, an invariant preserved exactly rather than merely bounded, proven composability, and an explicit terminal certificate yielding a universal constant. What keeps this from being simply "the answer" is entirely about verification status, not about the shape of the board: a single non-peer-reviewed preprint, four days old, with an AI system credited as having found the proof, and one independent technical concern already raised about its most delicate step.

My own independent track: BOARD CHANGE CANDIDATE, at best. The exponential-potential board (C2) is a real candidate with a concrete proposed move and a genuine (if modest) state/move simplification, but I found — and consider this the honest, useful output of pursuing it — that its crucial transformation does not obviously evade the cross-row-covariance obstruction that affine spectral independence exists to solve; it relocates the difficulty rather than removing it. Per the mission's Section 16, I'd rather report that plainly than dress it up.

Final research question
"What is the simplest new mathematical representation in which a useful Komlós move becomes almost stupid?"

Based on everything above, the honest answer is not my own construction — it's the one the literature search turned up. State: a bounded open convex set together with a probability density whose total variation is bounded by the same number κ in every direction at once. Move: an open-set Banaszczyk transform by the next vector. Invariant: that same κ, unchanged. Terminal condition: after all vectors are absorbed, the body is still symmetric and convex, so it contains the origin, which unwinds into an explicit sign for every vector. What makes the move "almost stupid" is that incorporating a vector costs nothing in the invariant — there is no accounting, no threshold, no row classification, no union bound over rows or over directions (the "all directions at once" part is handled once, up front, by a convex-separation argument, not re-proved per step). That is a stronger property than anything in Bansal–Jiang's board, which has to continuously re-certify which rows are dangerous and pay a union-bound tax to do it.

The honest caveat is exactly as sized as the claim: this simplicity is bought by a hard piece of geometric measure theory (an anisotropic Cheeger-constant convexity argument under Minkowski interpolation, pushed through a degenerate-norm limit) doing, in one shot, what Bansal–Jiang's apparatus does incrementally and with visible bookkeeping — and an independent reader has already pointed at the seam in that one-shot argument as the place most likely to need repair. I could verify the seed computation feeding into it (Section D1) and it held up cleanly; I could not verify the seam itself, and neither, as of this writing, has anyone else in public.
