CORRECTION. The gap is in the instrument, not in mathematics
==========================================================================
  This arm first concluded that under M4 'two independent refutations can
  both exist, neither be published, and the dependent literature never
  learn.' The operator's own case refutes that. What actually happened:
    - the researcher who found a counterexample first WROTE A PREPRINT;
    - the author of the disproven conjecture received messages from BOTH
      researchers, independently.
  So the result propagated through ordinary scholarly channels, to the one
  party with the most expertise and the most incentive to find a flaw in it.
  It simply did not make headlines, and so it is absent from the corpora
  this programme has been measuring. The failure is in the INSTRUMENT.

  That inverts the concern. Not 'mathematics may not learn' but 'the
  AI-progress measurement layer systematically undercounts', and
  specifically undercounts the unglamorous, load-bearing contributions.
  P2 above measures exactly that bias from the inside.

The schema has no tier for the state the operator's case reached
==========================================================================
  verification tiers, whole corpus / among disproofs:
    unreviewed         418     138
    lean-verified      137      34
    site-confirmed      64      24
    lean-checked        30       4
    expert-verified     13       5
    contested            2       0

  138 of 205 disproofs (67%) are `unreviewed`.

  There is no tier meaning 'the author of the refuted conjecture has been
  notified and accepts it'. That state is:
    - strictly stronger than `unreviewed` -- the person best placed to find
      a flaw has looked;
    - not the same as `expert-verified`, which in this corpus means someone
      reviewed the PROOF. The one entry that comes close, 'Chip-firing middle
      stair', earns `expert-verified` because two of the conjecture's posers
      helped REVIEW the argument -- a different and rarer event.

  So `unreviewed` conflates 'nobody has looked' with 'the one person with the
  most expertise and the most incentive to object has looked and agreed'.
  Across 138 disproofs that is a large amount of hidden variance, and the
  operator's case shows the stronger state occurs and goes unrecorded.

  Being told INDEPENDENTLY BY TWO researchers, with two different
  counterexamples, is stronger still -- arguably the best practical
  verification a refutation can have short of formalization. The schema
  cannot express it at all.
  PASS  the overstatement is corrected in place, not quietly dropped
  PASS  `unreviewed` is shown to conflate two very different epistemic states   [67% of disproofs sit in the conflated tier]
