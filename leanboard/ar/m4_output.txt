Verdict on M4
==========================================================================
  P1  NULL

  THE M4 SIGNATURE IS NOT DETECTABLE IN THIS CORPUS. One prediction is
  refuted in the opposite direction, two are null, one is marginal, and the
  one that passes overwhelmingly does so by definition.

  This does NOT refute M4, and the reason matters: the corpus cannot see it.
    1. M4's own logic says these refutations are working byproducts, and
       byproducts of an unfinished attack on a famous problem are exactly
       what does not get written up. The corpus indexes published claims.
    2. P2 measures that selection directly -- refutations here skew toward
       FAMOUS conjectures (p = 0.009). A corpus assembled from newsworthy
       results would hide the obscure-auxiliary class by construction.
    3. The decisive field does not exist in the schema: what the session was
       ORIGINALLY after. Nothing here distinguishes a conjecture attacked on
       purpose from one met on the way to something else.

  WHAT WOULD TEST IT: process artifacts, not outcome records. The Nevanlinna
  transcript in arm AQ is the only one this programme has seen, and one
  transcript is not a rate. A corpus field for the session's initial target,
  or a sample of raw sessions, would settle it.

  CONSEQUENCE, which holds whatever the rate turns out to be: under M1-M3 the
  colliding artifacts sit on a famous problem, so the community eventually
  looks. Under M4 the collision is on an obscure conjecture nobody is
  watching, which may have been load-bearing for conditional results for
  twenty years. Two independent refutations can both exist, and neither be
  published, and the dependent literature never learn.
  PASS  the null result is reported as a null result
