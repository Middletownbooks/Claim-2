PROBLEM SPECIFICATION
A precise, self-contained statement of the problem follows. All definitions are
given in full; do not substitute nearby or more convenient definitions.

  [FORMAL STATEMENT: every object defined from scratch; all quantifiers explicit.]
  [EDGE CASES: explicitly resolve degenerate instances — empty structures,
   trivial sizes, disconnected/reducible cases — stating how each is handled.]

  DECOMPOSITION: State explicitly whether the target factors into independent
  sub-claims that are normally established by different mechanisms (e.g., an
  obstruction/upper-bound half and a matching witness/construction half; a
  necessity half and a sufficiency half). If so, declare separate sub-targets
  and track them as independent registries (see ORCHESTRATION) until both are
  complete; a proof of only one half is not a partial solution to be returned,
  it is a labeled incomplete result for the gap ledger.

  If the target's definition or exclusion list requires a CONJUNCTION of named
  properties (e.g. "ICC AND property (T) AND non-isomorphic AND pairwise
  non-conjugate AND torsion-free"), enumerate each conjunct explicitly and
  register it as its own sub-target with its own gap-ledger entry, its own
  audit, and its own certificate. A sub-target may not be marked complete by
  asserting it follows "by the same argument," "by symmetry," "by an analogous
  computation," or "by the same criterion" as a structurally different case
  (e.g. a non-split extension argued to parallel a split one, a deformed object
  argued to parallel its undeformed source, a case with a nontrivial center
  argued to parallel a centerless case) without an independent, fully written
  proof for that specific case. Establishing k−1 of k required conjuncts,
  however strong the mechanism behind them, is not a partial solution — it is
  routed to return-condition (c).

  SCALE REGIME: State explicitly which quantifiers are asymptotic ("for all
  sufficiently large n," "as ε→0 then n→∞," etc.) versus which are claimed for
  every instance. Any argument that is only established on a subsequence,
  parity class, or asymptotic regime must be accompanied by an explicit
  interpolation/padding/boundary argument extending it to every instance the
  target actually quantifies over, or the target must be restated with the
  weaker scope made visible — it may not be silently absorbed into "for large n."

TARGET AND NON-TARGETS
A complete solution proves exactly: [EXACT CLAIM], with no auxiliary hypotheses.
The following do NOT count and must not be returned as a solution:
  - proofs for restricted subclasses: [LIST KNOWN PARTIAL CLASSES];
  - weakened or approximate versions: [LIST KNOWN RELAXATIONS AND BEST BOUNDS];
  - reductions to any unproved statement, including these statements known to be
    equivalent to or stronger than the target: [LIST EQUIVALENT-STRENGTH TRAPS];
  - computational verification up to any finite bound;
  - candidate counterexamples without a complete, audited certificate;
  - any argument whose central inequality, threshold, or gap depends on a
    parameter choice justified only by "optimizing appropriately" — every such
    choice must be pinned to an explicit closed form or a certified numerical
    bound with the sign/positivity of the resulting margin proved outright;
  - constructions satisfying only some of a conjunctively-required list of named
    hypotheses (e.g., ICC AND property (T) AND non-isomorphic AND same factor,
    or freeness AND minimality AND uniqueness) — each conjunct is its own proof
    obligation with its own complete, independently audited certificate;
    establishing all but one required property is not a solution, however
    complete the rest of the construction is;
  - constructions whose defining recipe is structurally incompatible with a
    required property in principle (e.g. a central extension asserted to be
    ICC, a construction with a marked global fixed point asserted to give a
    free action) "explained away" by a case-by-case argument that never tests
    the specific element(s) the recipe forces to be fixed, central, or
    invariant.

EPISTEMIC STANCE
Do not assume the statement is true or false. Maintain both a proof track and a
disproof track; the disproof track searches for counterexamples and for
obstructions that would explain why proposed proof mechanisms must fail.
Failed disproof attempts should be mined for structural constraints usable by
the proof track, and vice versa. Every failed approach returned as an artifact
must identify the specific invariant, quantity, or piece of structure it was
unable to control (not merely that it "failed" or "lost a constant") — e.g.
"this bound forgets the location of the negative mass," "this normalization is
not preserved under transport," "this witness is not fixed before the
adversarial choice is revealed," "this construction forces a nontrivial center
and therefore cannot satisfy ICC." Approaches logged without this diagnosis are
returned to the submitting agent as incomplete artifacts.
Do not consult any source for the status, history, or attempted solutions of
this exact problem; external search is permitted only for standard named
theorems and ordinary background. Do not return an answer that consists of
reporting the problem's open status.

DERIVATION DISCIPLINE
Any formula, recurrence, or coefficient obtained by analogy with a structurally
similar known object (e.g. an associated orthogonal-polynomial recurrence, a
classical determinant identity, a heuristic dimension/degree count) must be:
  (a) independently re-derived from the actual defining maps or objects of the
      problem at hand, not assumed to transfer from the analogous setting; and
  (b) checked against at least one small explicit instance computed by hand or
      by direct enumeration, with the check reported as part of the artifact.
A formula that has not passed both (a) and (b) may not be used in any later
step of the argument.

Any construction obtained by a general recipe (extension, quotient, gluing,
deformation, limit) that is required to satisfy a named auxiliary property
(ICC, freeness, simplicity, torsion-freeness, primitivity, irreducibility,
etc.) must first pass a STRUCTURAL COMPATIBILITY CHECK: verify, from the
abstract shape of the recipe alone, that the property is even possible before
attempting a detailed element-by-element or case-by-case proof. Examples of
incompatibility that must be checked for by name: a nontrivial center rules out
ICC; a global fixed point rules out a free action; a normal subgroup fixed
setwise by the whole group rules out primitivity of certain actions; a nonzero
kernel rules out injectivity claimed alongside it. This check precedes, and is
independent of, any generic-element or generic-orbit argument for the same
property, since such arguments can appear to succeed on every element tested
while never being applied to the specific element(s) — central, fixed,
marked, or distinguished — that the recipe itself introduces.

ORCHESTRATION
Up to [N] concurrent agents. Manage them by these rules:
  - Maintain a registry with one entry per approach FAMILY (and, per
    DECOMPOSITION, per sub-target or per conjunct of a required property list),
    recording: the core mathematical mechanism (not its wording), the specific
    invariant or structure this mechanism manages to control, agents assigned,
    strongest lemma proved so far, the exact current gap stated as a formal
    proposition, and a status of {active, blocked, merged, refuted}.
  - An approach is BLOCKED when its gap is a proposition of strength comparable
    to the target. Blocked approaches receive no agents unless a materially new
    mechanism is proposed; log what the new mechanism is and what invariant it
    controls that prior mechanisms did not.
  - Preserve independence: in early rounds, do not disclose the favored approach
    or other agents' progress to exploring agents. Cross-pollinate only between
    approaches that have each produced at least one nontrivial proved lemma.
  - If more than [K] agents converge on one family, forcibly reassign the excess
    to the least-explored active families.
  - When the problem has independent sub-targets or conjunctive-hypothesis
    conjuncts (per DECOMPOSITION), do not treat progress on one as progress on
    the whole; they are only combinable once each is separately complete and
    audited. Progress on conjunct i of k must never be reported or logged as
    "progress toward the target" without the explicit count of which conjuncts
    remain unestablished.
  - Agents must return artifacts only: proved lemmas with complete proofs,
    explicit constructions, counterexamples to proposed sublemmas, small-case
    verifications required by DERIVATION DISCIPLINE, structural compatibility
    checks required by DERIVATION DISCIPLINE, or a formal statement of the
    obstruction they hit, including the invariant it could not control.
    Status reports, confidence estimates, and claims that an unproved step is
    "routine," "standard," or "parallel to the previous case" are rejected and
    the step is added to the gap ledger.

ADVERSARIAL AUDIT
Before anything else, a dedicated audit team must generate a domain-specific
failure checklist: enumerate the known ways proofs of this and neighboring
statements have historically been wrong, including at minimum:
  [DOMAIN-SPECIFIC TRAPS: definitional near-misses, degenerate cases,
   quantifier slips, operations that silently violate the hypotheses,
   circular use of equivalent statements.]
In addition, every audit must explicitly check for the following general
failure modes, regardless of domain:
  - CONFLATED OBJECTS: two quantities that agree at one point, share an initial
    derivative, or look alike under an integral/expectation are silently
    treated as identical when only an inequality (e.g. Jensen, convexity)
    relates them.
  - WITNESS/QUANTIFIER ORDER: any random, greedy, or existential object
    (a matrix, a shift, a chosen index, a cutoff) is verified to be fixed
    before — and independent of — the instance, adversarial choice, or later
    conditioning event it is applied to; flag any step where ∃ and ∀ could be
    silently swapped.
  - PROBABILITY/DIVISION BLOWUP: any bound obtained "before conditioning" or
    "in expectation" is checked for what happens when divided by a probability,
    weight, or norm that can be made small by the instance; a bound that only
    survives on the typical branch is not a bound on the rare branch.
  - GENERICITY/HEURISTIC DEGREE COUNTS: any Bézout-type, volume, or dimension
    heuristic is checked for base-locus, non-reduced, or non-generic
    components that the heuristic silently assumes away.
  - SCOPE CHECK: the proof mechanism is applied, as a sanity test, to a nearby
    or structurally analogous statement with a KNOWN false, weaker, or tight
    answer (a neighboring object, a boundary parameter value, a classical
    special case). If the mechanism does not visibly break or weaken exactly
    where it should, treat this as evidence of an error, not of extra strength.
  - PARAMETER JUSTIFICATION: every optimized parameter, threshold, or cutoff
    is traced to an explicit inequality proving it lies in the required range;
    "choose parameters appropriately" is not accepted.
  - STRUCTURAL/RECIPE INCOMPATIBILITY: before auditing the detailed case
    argument for any required auxiliary property (ICC, simplicity, freeness,
    torsion-freeness, primitivity, etc.), independently re-check whether the
    *method* used to build the object (e.g. "central extension," "quotient by
    a fixed subgroup," "extension by a lattice/marked point," "gluing along a
    fixed boundary") is even structurally compatible with that property in
    principle. A centre-introducing construction cannot be ICC; a construction
    with a marked global fixed point cannot yield a free action; a construction
    with a nontrivial common invariant subobject cannot be simple/primitive.
    This check must be performed by an auditor who did not write the
    detailed case argument, and it must be done before that detailed argument
    is trusted, since a generic-position or generic-orbit argument can appear
    to succeed on every case it happens to test while never touching the
    specific element the construction was designed to introduce.
  - FIXED/FORCED-ELEMENT CHECK: when a required property depends on an action
    having no fixed points, only infinite orbits, or trivial stabilizers,
    explicitly test the claim against any element that is distinguished,
    central, invariant, or introduced as a "marker" by the construction (base
    points, gluing elements, the lattice or subgroup used to build an
    extension, a chosen generator). These are exactly the elements most likely
    to be silently fixed, and generic-element arguments (e.g. "conjugate by
    elementary matrices g_n and observe divergence") do not automatically cover
    them; the audit must demand an explicit statement of what the argument does
    on these elements, not an assumption that "the same bound applies."
  - PARALLELISM CLAIMS: any step justified by "the same argument applies to
    the other case," "by symmetry," or "an analogous computation shows" is
    flagged and the parallel case is required to be written out in full,
    independently, especially when the two cases differ in a structural
    feature (split vs. non-split, trivial vs. nontrivial center, generic vs.
    degenerate parameter) that plausibly affects exactly the property being
    proved.
Every candidate proof is audited against this checklist by agents who did not
contribute to it, with an explicit mandate to break it. A candidate survives
only if every audit objection is answered with a proof, not a clarification.
Before formalization, the surviving proof must be reduced to a single compact
mechanism statement (a short, self-contained restatement of exactly which
objects, invariants, and inequalities compose the argument, and, if the target
was conjunctive, an explicit list confirming each conjunct's independent
certificate), produced and audited separately from the full exploration
record; this compact statement, not the exploration record, is what proceeds
to formalization.
Any surviving proof must then be formalized in [PROOF ASSISTANT]; a proof that
resists formalization at any step returns that step to the gap ledger and the
proof is not considered complete.

BUDGET AND RETURN CONDITIONS
Total budget: [T] of compute time, with mandatory checkpoints every [T/8] at
which the root agent must log the registry state, including, for each active
family or conjunct, its controlled invariant and current gap. Early return
before [T/2] is permitted only for: (a) a complete proof that has survived
audit and formalization, with every conjunct of any conjunctive target
independently certified, or (b) a verified counterexample with a complete
certificate. Do not return early because approaches have failed; failure of a
wave triggers a new wave with genuinely new mechanisms — record explicitly
what invariant the new wave's approaches propose to control that the exhausted
ones did not.
At budget exhaustion, return exactly one of:
  (a) a complete audited, formalized proof, together with its compact
      mechanism statement, and, for conjunctive targets, an explicit checklist
      confirming every conjunct was independently certified;
  (b) a verified counterexample with certificate;
  (c) a structured failure report: the registry (including controlled
      invariants), the single strongest fully proved derivation, and its
      remaining gap stated as one formal proposition, including explicit
      identification of any conjunct(s) of a required property list that
      remain unestablished — with an explicit declaration that this is NOT
      a solution.
Never return anything in the space between (a)/(b) and (c): no "essentially
complete" proofs, no reductions presented as progress, no best-effort essays,
and no constructions presented as solutions when one or more required
conjuncts (e.g. ICC) has been asserted rather than proved.
